How to explain autism to your partner

I'm Sam, I'm neurodivergent, and I run Frog Logic from my lounge. Explaining autism to someone you love is different from explaining it to a relative. They're in your daily life, so the useful bit isn't the definition, it's the everyday stuff they'll actually see.

Start with what they'll notice

Skip the textbook and describe your own life: "When I go quiet after a busy day, I'm not upset with you, I'm out of energy." "Changes to plans hit me harder than they look." "I sometimes miss what's meant if it isn't said straight." Real examples land better than a list of traits.

The things most worth explaining

  • Shutdowns and recovery time. Needing quiet isn't rejection.
  • Sensory stuff. Noise, light, textures or touch that are fine for them may be a lot for you.
  • Plans and change. Knowing what's happening helps; surprises can cost a lot.
  • Communication. You may prefer things said plainly, in writing, or with time to answer.
  • Masking. Coping well in public often means you're more tired at home.

What to ask for

  • Say it straight: "Tell me directly if something's wrong, I won't guess."
  • A heads-up before plans change, where possible.
  • A signal you both understand for "I need some quiet".
  • Asking before touch when you're overloaded.

Questions people ask

How do I tell my partner I'm autistic?

Pick a calm moment, keep it short, and give one or two real examples from your life together. Offer them something to read afterwards so they can take it in at their own pace.

Can autistic people have happy relationships?

Yes. What usually helps most is clear, direct communication and agreeing on the everyday things like quiet time, plans and touch.

What should my partner know about autism?

That quiet time isn't rejection, sensory things can be intense, changes to plans cost energy, and plain words help more than hints.
