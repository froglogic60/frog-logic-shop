Christmas with an autistic child

I'm Sam, I'm neurodivergent, and I run Frog Logic from my lounge. Christmas is lots of things at once: new food, noise, visitors, presents, late nights and no routine. For many autistic children that's a lot. A calmer day usually comes from planning, not from trying harder on the day.

Before the day

  • Share the plan in advance, in pictures or words, and stick to it where you can. There's a free Christmas visual timetable to print.
  • Put decorations up gradually, or let your child help choose.
  • Agree with relatives beforehand what will help (you can send them a guide).

On the day

  • Keep a quiet room your child can go to without being followed.
  • Let them eat what they eat. A safe plate is fine.
  • Open presents a few at a time, or wrap them in clear paper so there are no surprises.
  • Ear defenders, a tablet or a favourite activity are tools, not bad manners.
  • Keep visits shorter than you think you need to, and have an exit plan.

After

Expect some fallout the next day or two. Plan a quiet one.

For the relatives

If you'll spend the day explaining your child, the free guide below does it for you, written for grandparents, aunties and uncles. There's also a page on explaining autism to grandparents.

Questions people ask

How can I make Christmas easier for my autistic child?

Share the plan in advance, keep a quiet space, let them eat safe foods, open presents gradually and keep visits short.

Should autistic children open presents in front of everyone?

Only if they're comfortable. Many find it easier to open a few at a time, or privately.

How do I explain my child's needs to relatives at Christmas?

Tell them a few clear things you need before the day, and send them a short written guide to read on their own.
