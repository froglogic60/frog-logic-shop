Free Christmas visual timetable

I'm Sam, I'm neurodivergent, and I run Frog Logic from my lounge. Christmas Day throws the usual routine out of the window. Knowing what comes next, in pictures, takes a lot of the worry out of it. This is the timetable I'd want: simple, calm, and free.

Download the timetable

Three pages, free, no email address, no sign-up. Print it, cut out the cards and stick them on with Blu Tack or Velcro dots.

Need more cards? The Big Pack (£3) has 60 picture cards (church, car, crackers, the dog walk and 12 blanks), a now and next board and a holiday-week planner.

What's in it

  • My Christmas Day: eight slots, First, Then and Last, each with a tick box.
  • 20 picture cards: wake up, presents, quiet time, visitors, car journey, Christmas dinner, video call, bath, bed and more, plus a blank one for anything special.
  • First, then board: for the moments when the whole day is too much to look at.
  • I need a break card: to show instead of having to find the words.
  • A note for the grown-ups.

How to use it

  • Go through it the day before, and again in the morning.
  • If plans change, change the cards and show the new order. A surprise on paper is easier than a surprise out loud.
  • Let the break card work without questions.
  • Laminate the cards, or print on card, and you can use them every year.

Questions people ask

What is a visual timetable?

A list of the day's activities shown in pictures, in order, so a child can see what's happening now and what comes next.

Do visual timetables help autistic children at Christmas?

Many autistic children find unpredictability hard. Seeing the day in advance, and seeing changes on paper, can make it calmer.

Is this timetable free?

Yes. It's free to download, print and share, with no sign-up.
