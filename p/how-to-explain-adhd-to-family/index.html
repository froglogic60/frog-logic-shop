How to explain ADHD to your family

I'm Sam, I'm neurodivergent, and I run Frog Logic from my lounge. ADHD gets explained badly a lot: "can't sit still", "easily distracted", "everyone's a bit ADHD". If your family still thinks you just need to try harder, these are the points that tend to help.

The one-sentence version

"ADHD isn't a lack of attention, it's trouble steering it. I can't make my brain start, stop or switch just because something matters."

What it looks like in real life

  • Time blindness. Five minutes and an hour can feel the same. Being late is not not caring.
  • Starting is the hard bit. Knowing what to do and being able to begin are different things.
  • Hyperfocus. The same brain that can't start the washing up can lose six hours to something interesting.
  • Out of sight, out of mind. If it's in a drawer, it may as well not exist.
  • Feelings run hot. Criticism and rejection can hit very hard.

What to ask your family for

  • Reminders without the sigh.
  • Body doubling: just being in the room while you do a boring task.
  • Written plans over verbal ones.
  • Credit for the effort, not only the result.

Questions people ask

How do I explain ADHD to my parents?

Keep it short: ADHD is trouble steering attention, not a lack of it. Give one or two real examples, say what helps, and offer them a short guide to read on their own.

Is ADHD just being lazy?

No. People with ADHD often try much harder than others for the same result. The difficulty is starting, switching and timing, not caring.

What can family do to help someone with ADHD?

Kind reminders, body doubling, written plans, and noticing effort as well as results.
